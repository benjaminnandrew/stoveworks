# "Will it fit my fireplace?": what the makers publish

Ben, 21 Oct 2026: "we could dig into this a little bit." Research only so far; nothing is built.

## The short version

**Update, 26 Oct 2026:** Stoveworks doesn't sell pellet, so the True North TN40 pellet insert
is off the site. A fit check would cover the other 27 inserts.

- **28 of the 29 inserts** on the site have published minimum fireplace sizes:
  - 26 come from the maker's own installation manual;
  - the 2 Kuma inserts come from Kuma's brochure and website, because the Kuma manuals only
    give the insert's own size.
- **The electric insert** (Kozy Heat Osseo eSeries) gives only a framed opening for building it
  into a wall. Its manual has no size for an existing fireplace, so it can't be checked the
  same way.
- **The guides already hold most of these figures.** They come from the owner & installer
  guides, which were number-checked against the manuals. Seven manuals give theirs only as a
  drawing or an image table, so I read those off the pages myself (marked † below). They need a
  second check before anything goes live.
- **The website figures aren't safe to use.** Two HearthStone inserts' websites disagree with
  their manuals; details are under "Where the makers' websites disagree". The manual figures are
  the ones to use.

## What a homeowner would measure

The makers measure up to five things. Every maker gives front width, height and depth; the rest
vary.

| # | Measurement | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Front width**: across the opening, at the face | all 28 |
| 2 | **Rear width**: across the back wall (most fireboxes taper) | 18 of 28 |
| 3 | **Height**: hearth floor to the top of the opening, at the front | all 28 |
| 4 | **Rear height**: at the back wall (many fireplaces slope the back) | 7 of 28 (the five Jøtul inserts, HearthStone Clydesdale, RSF) |
| 5 | **Depth**: front of the opening to the back wall, at the floor | all 28 |

Some minimums depend on how the insert is set up:

- **Depth.**
  - HearthStone GMi 70 and GMi 90 need less depth with their surround extension or projection kit.
  - Mendota FV33i needs less with a deeper surround edge.
  - The tool can say "fits with the surround extension".
- **Width.** HearthStone suggests at least 4″ more width where a fresh-air kit is fitted.
- **Height.**
  - Kozy Heat Nordik: add ½″ when the floor-protector kit is used.
  - Morsø 5660 B: the blower version needs 26″, the standard 21¼″ (they're two records).
- **Lintel.** HearthStone Clydesdale also limits how deep the lintel can be: 9 11/16″ at 24½″
  height, or 7 11/16″ at 23 3/16″. That's one more measurement, for one model.

## The fireplace type matters as much as the size

Each manual says which kinds of fireplace the insert can go in:

- **Masonry only:** Blaze King (3), Jøtul C 550, Pacific Energy (3), HearthStone (3), RSF, Kuma
  (2), True North TN20.
- **Masonry, or the maker's own enclosure:** Morsø (2), which can go in a masonry fireplace or
  Morsø's own zero-clearance enclosure.
- **Masonry or factory-built (metal, zero-clearance):** Kozy Heat Chaska and Nordik (6), Jøtul GI
  gas (4), Mendota FV33i, and True North TN40 pellet ("size permitting").
- **The manual contradicts itself:** Pacific Energy (all three), True North TN20, HearthStone
  Clydesdale, RSF and the Morsø 5660 B standard. Each says "not in a factory-built fireplace" in one place and mentions
  factory-built in another (rating label, clearance text or front page). The guides follow the
  installation text, so treat these as masonry only.

The tool's first question should be "brick or stone fireplace, or a metal factory-built one?",
with "not sure" as an answer. A masonry-only insert should never show as a fit for a
factory-built fireplace.

## Where the makers' websites disagree

| Insert | Website | Manual |
|---|---|---|
| HearthStone Clydesdale | 32½ wide × 23¼ high × 13 deep | 31⅝ front / 24 rear wide, 23 3/16 high (21 at the rear), 13 deep |
| HearthStone GMi 70 | 29 × 24 × 16 | 28⅞ × 23 5/16 × 19⅝ (15⅝ is the smallest, with the surround extension) |
| HearthStone GMi 90 | matches (31¾ × 27⅛ × 22⅛) | matches; the site's footnote says it's "with surround extension", but the manual gives 22⅛ without the kit |

The rest that list a size on their websites match their manuals. The GMi 70 case shows why the
website figures shouldn't be used: a 17″-deep fireplace passes on the website's 16″, but the
manual needs 19⅝″ unless the surround extension is fitted.

## A possible tool

**Where:** on /inserts/, under "Choose a fuel", and linked from the Wood and Gas Inserts pages.

**Asks:**

1. Brick/stone fireplace, a metal factory-built one, or not sure.
2. Front width, height and depth. These are required.
3. Rear width and rear height. These are optional, with "my fireplace tapers" revealing them.

A small drawing shows where each tape measure goes.

**Shows each insert as one of three:**

- **Fits the maker's minimums.** If it fits only with an add-on, the result says which one, e.g.
  "with the surround extension".
- **Close.** Short by an inch or less; the result says what's short and by how much. Kozy Heat
  allows removing firebrick or a smoke shelf to reach its minimums, but that's for an installer
  to judge, so these stay "close" rather than "fits".
- **Too small**, or **not for this fireplace type**.

A measurement the visitor skipped is listed as "check your rear width", never assumed to fit.
Each result links to that insert's guide at its "Minimum fireplace opening" card, where the
manual page opens.

**What it doesn't check:**

- the chimney: liner size, height, condition;
- the hearth and floor protection;
- clearances to the mantel and walls;
- the gas line;
- the weight.

The page has to say so plainly. Steve should word that, and whether the visitor brings their
measurements in, sends a photo, or books a visit. Those are his business claims.

**Data:** these figures belong in the brain, next to the rest of each model's specs, as a
`fireplace_minimums` block on each insert:

- front and rear width, front and rear height, depth;
- the add-on variants;
- the allowed fireplace types;
- the manual page.

The brain's tooling can then number-check them the way the guides were checked, and
`brain:load` brings them to the site. The brain is Ben's, so it's his call. The alternative is
a checked data file in the site.
